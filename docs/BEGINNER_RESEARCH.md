# KEYFRAME: beginner curriculum and discovery research

Research date: September 29, 2026.

## What was checked

21 specific videos across 11 channels. All 21 were public and embeddable when checked through YouTube Data API; titles, durations, publication dates, view counts, and channel identities were verified. None of their video IDs occur in the existing 525-video public export. Availability is not a guarantee that playback works in every region.

This is a desk-researched editorial selection using original creator pages, video descriptions, chapter lists, and current YouTube metadata. It is **not an end-to-end viewing review**. Teaching clarity, spoken language, captions, asset access, and exact plugin requirements still need manual playback confirmation where not established in the sources. No subscriber threshold by itself proves quality. “Underrated” here means a useful candidate with relatively little discovery, not an objective quality score.

## Curriculum decisions

The new /start page gives beginners 12 ordered lessons. The first 11 total 107 minutes of video; the final project adds 135 minutes. Practice time is additional. Learners do not need to finish it in one sitting.

Sequence: setup → organization → keyframes → pivots → easing → shapes → text → parenting → masks → mattes → export → project. These order choices and exercises are editorial recommendations.

Megan Friesth’s Animation Explained lessons form the core because the original [AE Fundamentals hub](https://www.meganfriesth.com/aefundamentals) provides short topic lessons with written guides. MiddyAtif adds a focused graph-editor explanation; TipTut supplies a capstone after the basics. The hub’s optional download kit is separate from watching the linked YouTube lessons.

## Admission rules for the next batch

1. Confirm AE relevance, actual video ID, availability, and creator identity.
2. Watch the introduction and an instructional segment; verify pacing, readable UI, spoken language, and captions. Watch the whole video before calling it fully reviewed.
3. Identify prerequisites; do not infer “absolute beginner” from a title containing “beginner.”
4. Separate native tools from optional convenience scripts and required paid plugins.
5. Record software-version differences (particularly mattes and H.264 export).
6. Assign a concrete exercise and one skill outcome.
7. Check duplicates by YouTube ID and compare with existing lessons.
8. Prefer complementary explanations, not ten near-identical tutorials.
